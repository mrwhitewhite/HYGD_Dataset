# Hillel Yaffe Glaucoma Dataset (HYGD)
> A Gold-Standard Annotated Fundus Dataset for Glaucoma Detection
https://physionet.org/content/hillel-yaffe-glaucoma-dataset/1.0.0/

# What is Glaucomatous optic neuropathy (GON) 
1. A chronic, progressive neurodegenerative disease 
2. Characterized by the loss of retinal ganglion cells and their axon
3. Resulting in distinct structural changes to the optic nerve head and corresponding visual field defects.
4. It can cause irreversible blindness

# Symptoms of GON
1. Increased Cup-to-Disc Ratio
2. Neuroretinal Rim Thinning
3. "Notching" and Haemorrhages
4. Vascular Shifting (Bending Vessels)



# Why we need AI
> To identify Glaucoma, you need an expensive machines to produce high-res photos of the back of your eye(Digital Fundus Images). Then, doctor will identify whether an eye looks "glaucomatous". 

1. **Problem:** Looking at a photo is subjective, different doctor may have different opininions on same image. Most existing AI datasets use labels based only on what a doctor saw in the photo. If the doctor was wrong or the photo was blurry, the AI "learns" those mistakes.
2. **Solutions:** The Hillel Yaffe Glaucoma Dataset (HYGD) is special because the labels (GON+ or GON-) aren't just guesses based on photos. In HYGD, the label means "this patient definitely has Glaucoma according to every medical test available." This makes the model trained from the dataset be more accurate

# Features

## Labels
- **GON+ (Glaucomatous):** This image belongs to a patient who has confirmed Glaucoma based on that year-long clinical study.
- **GON- (Non-Glaucomatous):** This image belongs to a "control" patient who was examined and found to be healthy (or at least free of Glaucoma).

## Quality Score
1. This is a unique numerical value from 1 to 10 assigned to every image. 
2. It was generated by another AI called **FundusQ-Net**
3.  It measures Clarity, focus, illumination, and whether the important parts of the eye (like the Optic Disc) are visible and summarise into a score.
4. Inteprete the score:
    - 1–3 (Low Quality): Blurry, dark, or out of focus. It's hard even for a human to see what’s going on.
    - 4–7 (Average Quality): Most of the dataset falls here (Mean is ~5.9). These are "real-world" clinical images.
    - 8–10 (High Quality): Crystal clear, professional-grade images.
